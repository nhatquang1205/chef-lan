# Video player page — spec

Our own watch page in `chef-lan-web`, instead of sending the visitor to Bunny's raw embed URL in a new
tab. It shows the video inside our layout, knows which course and lesson it belongs to, and applies the
"free until N seconds" limit itself.

## Decisions (proposed — see "Open items")

| | |
|---|---|
| Player | **Bunny's embedded player in an `<iframe>`, controlled with Bunny's player.js API**. Not a hand-built HLS player |
| URL | `/courses/{slug}/watch/{videoId}` (`/en/...` in English) |
| Signed URL | fetched **when the page opens** from a dedicated endpoint, not carried in the course outline |
| Labels | free video with `freeTillSeconds = null` -> **WATCH VIDEO**; free with a limit -> **WATCH PREVIEW**; not free -> locked, no button |
| Limit | enforced **in the browser** — a teaser limit, not a paywall (see below) |

## Can `freeTillSeconds` be enforced on our player page?

**Yes in the page, no on the server — and that difference matters.**

The Bunny embed exposes a player.js API: the page can read the current time, listen to `timeupdate`,
call `pause()` and `setCurrentTime()`. So our page can:

- stop playback when the time reaches `freeTillSeconds` and show "the preview has ended"
- pull the playhead back if the viewer drags the timeline past the limit
- cover the player with our own message and a way to get the full course

What it **cannot** do: stop a determined viewer. The iframe's `src` is a signed link to the *whole*
video, and it is visible in the page. Someone who opens that link directly, or uses the browser's dev
tools, plays everything until the link expires (`Video:Bunny:TokenLifetimeSeconds`, 2 hours today). Bunny's
tokens only bind a link to a video and an expiry; they have no "first N seconds" field. A real server-side
limit would need a separately encoded preview clip (an ffmpeg step per video), which was ruled out earlier
as too much machinery for a teaser.

Ways to raise the bar without building that:

- keep the signed lifetime short (the player re-requests a link when the page reloads)
- in the Bunny library's **Security** settings, allow playback only from our domain, and block direct
  URL access, so a link opened outside our page stops working (verify this behaves as expected before relying on it)
- do not use `freeTillSeconds` for anything that must stay paid: mark that video `isFree = false`

So: it is a good, honest **teaser limit**, and exactly as strong as the existing spec says ("enforced by
the frontend"). What this plan adds is that it works on our page rather than in Bunny's tab.

## Why the iframe, not our own HLS player

A fully custom player (hls.js plus our own controls) is possible but costs a lot for little gain here:
it needs the library's CDN hostname and Bunny's *other* token scheme (CDN token authentication, a
different formula from the embed token already built), our own controls, captions, quality switching and
mobile quirks. The iframe plus player.js gives branding, layout and the limit for a fraction of the work.
Revisit only if the Bunny player's look or behaviour becomes a real problem.

## What each side needs

**Backend**: today `GET /api/v1/courses/{slug}` puts a signed URL on every free video in the outline. The
watch page should instead ask for the link when it opens:
`GET /api/v1/videos/{id}/playback` -> `{ url, title, isFree, freeTillSeconds, expiresAt, courseSlug,
lessonTitle }`, answering 403 unless the video is free (full access arrives with enrollments) and 404 for
unpublished or unknown videos. The resolver, entitlement rule and signing already exist.

**Web**: a watch route, the iframe, the player.js controller, the overlay, and the new button labels.

## Decided and built

- **Signed link moved out of the course outline** (open item 1): the outline carries `publicId`, `isFree`
  and `freeTillSeconds` only; the link is minted per play by `GET /api/v1/videos/{publicId}/playback`.
- **Locked videos** (open item 2): a lock and "Enroll to watch" in the outline.
- **Public id** (asked later): videos are addressed publicly by an opaque 16-hex `public_id`, generated by
  Postgres (`gen_random_uuid()`), never the integer id and never the Bunny GUID. Existing rows were
  backfilled by the migration. Lessons and courses keep their slugs; only videos needed a new id.
- Bunny security settings (open item 4) remain a dashboard task for you; not done.

## Verified (local, mock player)

Backend by curl, web in the browser against a mock of Bunny's player.js API (the real player needs a real
Bunny video): the three outline buttons; the unlimited video loads no player.js and has no cover; a limited
video stays covered until the player is ready (fail closed) and its iframe is unreachable by keyboard; the
countdown; stop at the limit (pause, back to the limit, overlay); a seek past the limit is undone; a seek
inside the free part is not; a later `play` is paused again. **Not verified:** behaviour against a real Bunny
player (event names and timing are from Bunny's documentation), and Bunny's referrer/direct-link settings.

## Open items (remaining)

1. **Move the signed URL out of the course outline?** Recommended yes: the outline then carries flags
   only (`isFree`, `freeTillSeconds`), the link is minted fresh per play, and enrollment gating later
   lives in one place. Cost: a small breaking change to `GET /courses/{slug}` that the web must follow
   in the same release.
2. **Locked (paid) videos**: show a lock and "Contact chef Lan to enroll" in the outline? Today they show
   only the title.
3. **Where does "next video" go?** Same lesson's next video, then the next lesson's? (Nice to have;
   not in these plans.)
4. **Bunny Security settings** (allowed domains, direct URL blocking): they are dashboard-only changes,
   done by you, once the page exists and can be tested.
